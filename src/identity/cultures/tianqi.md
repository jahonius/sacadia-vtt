# The Tianqi


At the top of a giant, weathered, wall, a group of soldiers look north to a misty mountain valley dotted with ruins. The wall thrums with blue sigils of light, each taller in height than two men - the only mark of power on an otherwise crumbling stone edifice, pockmarked with recent repairs. The soldiers wearily watch for motion beyond their motes of torch and rune light. They are taciturn, eating stale bread in silence on their vigil. These men and women have seen death. These men and women are Tianqi.

## The Wallwatchers

The Tianqi are a society twisted by fate, bound to a pact and curse woven into their very geography. They are stoic, and dying, as they guard their corner of the world from a demonic menace that lies beyond. They are characterized by:

- **The Wall and War:** The Tianqi are at constant war with a demon lord obsessed with their destruction. Their war has whittled them away until little now remains, and now they guard their remaining cities from behind a massive wall.

- **Blood Glyphs:** The Tianqi infuse sigil-bound papers with blood magic through glyphic pact magic. These ‘blood glyphs’ have magic properties.

- **Isolationism:** Superstitious and stoic, the Tianqi are isolationist, and maintain a constant blockade against neighboring cultures wherever possible.

- **Blood Heroism:** Determined that the solution to their fate is the blood of great heroes, the Tianqi have tied great heroes to their religious and noble lineages, determined that their descendants might one day help.

## The Split Deadlands: A Tianqi History

The Tianqi are, like the Myrgha and Cunei Myrgha, descended from the Shau of Goldenstone.

### The Golden Diaspora

When the city of Goldenstone was at its peak, it colonized several surrounding regions, including the Heibrim. As Goldenstone was captured by Fengshaun the Demon King, the once-Shau colonies of the Heibrim drifted apart, becoming the Tianqi and Qianpo Shaugi.

### The Roots of the Heibrim

The mountains to the north of the Heibrim were found to be rich in minerals and gold, and the city of Qianpo, closest to this mineral wealth, prospered. The dependency of Golden Diaspora city gods on gold, and the crushing need Tianqi and Qianpo therefore shared for more, further pushed them to tensions, until eventually the Tianqi declared war on the city of Qianpo.

### The Siege of Qianpo

The wars that followed between the Tianqi and Qianpo culminated in a Tianqi siege of the city of Qianpo, which ended when the people of Qianpo turned upon one another and eat each other to survive. For this great sin, the people of Qianpo were cursed as demons, possessed with an unending hatred of the Tianqi. These demons spilled forth from the city of Qianpo into the armies of the Tianqi, routing them. Subsequently, these demons poured across the Heibrim, attacking and killing Qianpo city-states, and destroying all remaining armies of both Tianqi and Qianpo.

### The Sealings

In a last ditch effort to stop the demons of Qianpo, the surviving general of the Tianqi, Chunisaren of Kjerst, is said to have summoned a giant wall through the sacrifice of the Tianqi city gods, creating a grand wall of divine power that protects the Tianqi to this day from the northern hordes.

## Geography of the Heibrim

The Tianqi live in the Lower Heibrim, a highland valley that saddles the northeast border of the Ardus Yauga, and the northwest border of the Asher Yauga beyond. From the perspective of the Tianqi, however, the west-east divide means very little compared to their war.

### Chuni’s Wall

The Heibrim is split along Chuni’s Wall, an enormous wall spanning several day’s journey from Kjerstwall to Low Ballom. It is a hundred feet tall at its highest point, fifty at its lowest, and made of old, black-weathered tuff filled in with packed earth, sprouting small purple pigsqueak flowers along its sides. The wall is manned at all times, and it is held together by the runic blood arts the Tianqi practice to ward away demons. This magic wall is the main defense the dwindling Tianqi have against the north.

### Low Ballom

Low Ballom is the Capitol of the Heibrim, and the seat of power. Settled at the east sprawling edge of Chuni’s wall, it is home to the Goldwardens and the Monarch of all Tianqi, who dwells in the Glass Palace, which looks forever north beyond the wall, reminding the Monarch of their Sacred Duty in killing Ager.

### Kjerstwall

Kjerstwall is the second largest city in the Heibrim, and sits at the western edge of the wall, over the Lake of Xil. The city is home to the Redwardens, and shrimp farmers regularly trawl the lake for small krill which inhabit the salty green lake waters. Salt flats along the edge of the city harvest green and red salts, giving the city’s terraces vibrant green and pink hues, which contrast strongly with the city’s ruinous and worn-down buildings.

### Qianpo

Just north of Chuni’s Wall is Qianpo, a blasted land of demons. The demons of Qianpo (technically Folk Fae), prowl this land as beasts, imbued with immense divine power, but absent of any morals, and fixated with the consumption of human flesh. As long as the Tianqi lack the manpower and finances for a coordinated army, they are locked in an eternal struggle with these northern demons, forever sending small expeditionary bands north of the wall to attempt to kill demons, recover artifacts, and search for a lost city of gold (Footstool) that holds the key to their success (so say some).

# Cultural Tapestry

## Architecture

- **Mausoleums:** The center of each city is a vast mausoleum structure – a lengthy barrel vaulted hall that is built ever outwards and in a byzantine fashion. Nobles (of the priesthood) are buried on the ground floor, and commoners are buried in the tombs below.

- **Totems:** Tianqi architecture incorporates prayer totems directly into construction as towers and gates. Cylindrical wooden columns are incorporated regularly into construction so that villagers may place prayers on them accordingly.

## Arts and Music

- **Writing:** The principal form of arts in Tianqi is written works. In particular, Tianqi’s glyph papers have become an art form, with artistically decorated written prayers (particularly set in scrolls or woven into tapestries) considered a high art form. Tianqi works are meant to be clear and easily explained.

- **The Dead:** Artistic depictions of the dead or demons are common, and often interwoven into the glyph papers are depictions of heroes fighting demons. The Tianqi have many poems and stories that are told over and over again regarding the defeat of spectacular demons in myths passed down.

## Customs and Language

- **Language:** The dominant language of the Tianqis is Bushiu, though the variant spoken has characteristically old words and pronunciations, and sounds to other members of the Ardus Yauga similar to “old english”

- **Invitations:** There is a custom among the Tianqi to ask someone if they are a demon before letting them into a building, after a mythological tale in which a demon sneaks into a city to kill everyone.

## Economy and Trade

- **Erosion:** The Tianqis have been embroiled in a war with Ager for so long that it has eroded their international power. The Myrgha have claimed part of their land, and their trade is limited.

- **Limited Trade:** Tianqi trades through the port city of Tianqis with the cultures of the Friyu Yauga (to the east). They limit trade with the Myrgha and White Cunei due to cultural hatred of demons.

## Family Structure

- **Firstborn:** The firstborn child of each family is legally obligated to the military, regardless of sex.

- **Matriarchal:** The Tianqis familial structure is Matriarchal, and women hold great power as they are commonly more literate than men, and spend time writing the warding glyphs used by Tianqis.

- **Children:** Children are greatly valued as an obligation among the Tianqis, but due to the war with Ager, parents have to bury their children often, and so familial ‘closeness’ is usually only achieved after a child returns from war.

## Folklore and Superstitions

- **Superstitious:** The Tianqi are immensely superstitious, and most of their folklore surrounds demons of various kinds. They are particularly wary of anything mystical, and they have a strong tendency to prefer simple, normal lives.

- **Lexicon:** The Tianqi have tales and stories about each demon north of the wall who has been killed, or has killed. They have a lexicon of known demons.

- **Golden Touch:** Gold is seen as inherently corrupting due to its immense power, and only noble heroes are allowed to touch gold with bare skin, or hold onto it for any amount of time.

- **Warding Glyphs:** The Tianqi believe in the power of warding glyphs, and also that improper glyphs will incite demons further. Therefore, it is of great importance among the Tianqi to diligently hone their writing skills. Women are predominantly more literate than men.

## Food

- **Banana Yucca:** The Tianqi grow banana yucca (a sweet potato-esque yucca plant) in large quantities, as well as a form of juicy aloe whose leaves are edible and taste like soothing water.

- **Aloe:** The aloe leaves are brewed into a thick, creamy drink which is fermented and alcoholic, but tastes like cooling aloe. It is called Algon.

## Games and Leisure

- **Qianini:** A game is played among the Tianqi with small wooden pieces on a rectangular grid board called Qianini, in which they move around little wooden tokens for heroes and demons who fight. The game is based on myths told, and each hero kills a different demon (and vice versa), adding complication and variation to the game.

## Government

- **Divine Monarchy:** Tianqis is ruled over by an imperial, divinely empowered monarch. The ruling family embroils itself in consistent power struggles, but the leading authority of Tianqis is backed by a holy Cult of Glyphs which worships the progenitor heroes Chunisaren and Babelia, as well as Ascendant heroes who died fighting demons.

- **Cult of Glyphs:** The Cult of Glyphs holds many ruling authorities and is a judicial body in Tianqis. Crimes are judged through the church rather than through a civil body.

## Religion

- **Written Word:** The Tianqi believe strongly in the power of written word like the Shaugi, but they do not have city gods in the same sense as their ancestors. Instead, they believe in the power of great heroes who have been brought to power through the will of man.

- **Heroes:** The culture is greatly influenced by “Ascendant” heroes who killed one of the 11 Demon Generals, and most prominent among these heroes are Chunisaren and Babelia - who killed the first two, and established methods for killing demons.

- **Mausoleums:** The Tianqi believe in the power of cities, and the center of each city holds a vast mausoleum, which is the burial location of all citizens of the city. It is a massive tomb, and height and depth is added to the mausoleum as needed, so that it grows both up and down constantly.

- **Prayers:** In Tianqi tradition, prayers are placed for one week and then burned. More commonly required prayers may be sewn into fabric, which allows them to be placed for a decade before they must be replaced.

- **Writing:** The Cult of Glyphs maintains complex written records on behalf of the state, and they contort history according to their aims and goals.

## Societal Structure

- **Cult and Society:** The Cult of Glyphs holds a ruling role in society and is rarely questioned. It is said among the Tianqi that if the Cult of Glyphs did something wrong, they will die.

- **Two Lives:** the people in Tianqis are said to live two lives - their first, children and young adults training for and being involved in war. Their second, learning and practicing a trade. This especially holds for eldest children, but the weariness of war hangs over all in Tianqis.

## Values

- **Tales:** The Tianqi are superstitious people, and value stories of great heroes and the vanquishing of demons. They are very superstitious of outsiders and foreigners, and are relatively insular peoples.

- **Foreigners:** The Tianqi have a tendency not to like foreigners as a result of the challenges they have had (e.g., Fengshuan being a demon and claiming traditional Tianqi lands)

- **Normalcy:** The Tianqi value normalcy in their lives, and rarely get it.

## War

- **Ager:** The Tianqi are embroiled in war with Ager, the Cannibal King, and expend a significant amount of military effort against him. The Tianqi are militaristic, but after hundreds of years manning Chuni’s Walls and fighting Ager, they are tired of war, and soldiers are not treated well.

- **Glyphs:** The Cult of Glyphs runs an oracular cult who creates glyph papers of special, strongly hallowing properties. Oracles and Tianqi soldiers work together to sneak into the Heibrim in order to place these glyph papers in such a way that they stop the progress of demons.

## Plot Hooks

- **War Aid:** The city of Tianqis reflects the economic impact in the war with Ager in the many ruined buildings in the city – it is not a city on the rise. They would welcome trade and political opportunities that might support their efforts, and are desperate for solutions.

- **Glyph Cult:** the Glyphic Cult in Tianqis secretly consumes the flesh of demons, turning high-ranking priests into part-demons themselves. These priests train their whole lives to maintain their meditative ethos after this begins to affect their brain, and they seek to control the power of demons. But these soldiers are significantly more powerful than normal humans.
