# The Cunei Myrgha


The pulse of industry beats through the Cuneises. To the west lie vast white marble and chalk mines. To the right, rich black peat and black marble veins. In the center, an industrial powerhouse and political dance keeping these regions in check.

## Black and White

The Cunei Myrgha (often called simply the Cunei) differ from their southern kin by the beat of production and their focused work ethic, as well as a violent history of oppression and prejudice. They are most known for:

- **Bureaucracy:** The Cunei have a deeply legalist Bureaucracy. The ministers that run the state do so in a complex and byzantine legalist system.

- **Intrigue:** The Cuneises are formally separate government entities, but the complex interweave of bureaucracy and economy between them means the Cuneises are constantly embroiled in intrigue.

- **Artisanship and Agriculture:** The Cuneises are known for fine artisanal crafts of marble, and as the breadbasket of Myrghos.

## The Veins of Empire

The pulse of untapped potential beats through the Cuneises. To the west lie vast white marble and chalk mines. To the east, rich black peat and black marble veins. In the center, a tumultuous network of Legalist intrigue, struggling under the weight of outside influence. The Cunei are the industrial and agricultural center of Myrghos, and they differ from their southern kin by the beat of production and their focused work ethic. Though the Cunei are independent of the Myrgha, few cultures can readily mark the influence of outside empire like the Cunei. The Myrgha are constantly working beneath the surface in the Cuneis to keep them divided, dependent upon the Myrgha, and aligned to Oligarchic goals.

## A History of the Cunei Myrgha Colonies

When the demon Fengshuan killed his brother Gongshuan and scattered the Myrgha westward into the Soren Rou, his intent was to banish them to a backwater existence. Though he was correct regarding the feeble pickings of Soren Rou, the Myrgha found new life in their colonies in the Cuneises, a surprisingly mineral-rich peninsula to the south of Kish. Here, massive veins of white and black marble would become the symbol of Myrgha persistence and stubbornness. The black peat of the region would feed and sustain the Myrgha, who were able to take eastern agricultural practices to create a network of canals through the Black Cuneis to turn this once dead land into verdant farmland.

### Oligarchic Oppression

Though the Myrgha colonized the Cuneis, they ran a wealth extraction colony in the region, and as such, only one Oligarch (the White Queen, an immortal Courter) ever ruled over the Cuneises. This meant the Cunei Myrgha were constantly underrepresented and oppressed in Myrgha government.

### Rebellion

After the Myrgha war against Fengxi, the Cunei rebelled, and (supported by the Olganyar) able to claim their independence. Though the Myrgha had the resources to put down this rebellion, differing Myrgha interests quickly realized that they could each have a piece of Cunei interests by working against each other, and the Cuneises achieved a delicate political independence - provided they remained economically fully entwined with the Myrgha.

## Cunei Unique Locations

### The City of Whitecliff

The city of Whitecliff, the capitol of the White Cuneis, is carved out of a giant white marble mine. As a result, each building is not constructed, but carved from the raw marble directly. This port city to the north of the Cuneises houses the White Palace, an ornate marble and iron palace complex still home to the annointed descendants of the White Queen.

### Heinol, the Terraced City

The capitol of the Black Cuneis is Heinol. Similarly carved out of black marble, but carved into the side of a low mountain in a terraced complex, Heinol is a black stone and verdant green planted cityscape, representing the agricultural success of the region.

# Cultural Tapestry

## Architecture

- **Carved Cities:** In the Cuneises, architecture is monochromatic (black or white) based on the form of marble found in each area. Because of the great quantities of marble, several major Cunei cities (e.g., Heinol, Whitecliff) are carved out of Marble quarries over several centuries.

- **Home and Business:** Most homes are generational, and added onto over time. The core of each home is the working business, or trade, that is passed down through that family.

- **Subculture Variation:** The Architecture of the White and Black Cunieses varies greatly. See Subcultures for more details.

## Arts and Music

- **Sturdy and Simple:** Amongst Cunei, constructing something simple that lasts forever is valued. Cunei crafts tend towards simpler artistic art styles, but designed to last forever.

- **Horn Music:** The Cunei Myrgha design deep bass horns, which can be more felt than heard when they reverberate and sound. Music in general amongst the Cunei is designed to be felt as much as heard.

## Customs and Language

- **Language:** The official Cunei Myrgha language is Bushiu, but regional accents between Black and White Cuneises are obvious to the layman Cunei.

- **Tools at the Door:** In the Cuneises, it is a custom to leave one’s tools and trade at the door of their home after work. Travelers can then see what trade they practice and offer, while simultaneously, it lets them relax and focus on family in evenings.

## Economy and Trade

- **Economic Corruption:** The byzantine ministries of the Cuneises allow rampant Myrgha corruption, and the enormous value of Cunei marble and agricultural goods has encouraged this further. The Cunei economy has enabled a few to become rich, as long as they support Myrgha interests.

- **Subculture Variation:** The Economy of the White and Black Cunieses varies greatly. See Subcultures for more details.

## Family Structure

- **Servitude to Elders:** Amongst the Cunei Myrgha, serving one’s elder is seen as not only a blessing but as a calling to all Cunei. The Cunei see their elders as the wisest and most powerful, and they are highly subservient and respectful to those older than them.

- **Generational Homes:** Most Cunei Myrgha live and work within the home they grow up in as a child their whole lives - adding onto the home as needed for their own families. This is because Cunei Myrgha typically practice a trade or artisanry out of their home, and they pass down that trade to their children and take them on as apprentices.

## Folklore and Superstitions

- **Folk Fae:** The most pronounced Folk Fae in the Cuneises are Marblekin - massive Marble spirits that swim through marble and defend quarries. Another Folk Fae common in the Cuneises are Goat Eaters, which are spirits of the dead which consume goats in vast quantities.

- **Subculture Variation:** The Folklore of the White and Black Cunieses varies greatly. See Subcultures for more details.

## Food

- **Spice:** Both cuneises have a significant love of spices which outpaces that of their neighbors.

- **Subculture Variation:** The Food of the White and Black Cunieses varies greatly. See Subcultures for more details.

## Games and Leisure

- **Gambling:** Although gambling is illegal in the Cuneises, widespread illegal gambling takes place within the Cuneises. The competitive nature of Cunei Myrgha excels through gambling activities.

- **Stone Games:** Small stone games using black and white marble, similar to Go, provide an opportunity for players to test their skill. It also gives a convenient excuse to begin gambling.

## Government

- **Ministries of the Cuneises:** The Ministries of the Cuneises are a byzantine and complex network of legalist scholars, ministers over various functions, and lawmakers. The Ministry is broadly split into the five orders the Myrgha once used in the region (Policy, Economy, Measurement, Education, and Religion)

- **Pseudo-Oligarchy:** The Cunei ministries are constantly in internal struggle. The Ministries of Economy and Measurement are filled with ministers accepting corrupt Myrgha handouts for economic deals, with the other ministers working to combat Myrgha corruption. “Pseudo-Oligarchs” - powerful trade and economic ministers - have great power in the Cuneises.

- **Subculture Variation:** The Government of the White and Black Cunieses varies greatly. See Subcultures for more details.

## Religion

- **Ancester Worship:** The Cunei Myrgha follow the Rochi Shamanic Order, but in the Cuneises there is a much greater focus on ancestor-worship than the Myrgha proper. Ancestor-worship has a massive festival each year for the Cunei in a ceremony similar to their own “Day of the Dead”

## Societal Structure

- **Prejudice:** The Cunei Myrgha have deep-seated prejudice against one another, keeping their ministries separated (at least on the surface) and keeping the two subcultures at constant odds.

- **Tradesmen and Merchants:** Tradesmen and merchants are seen as having the greatest calling - to produce and to share. As a result, these are seen as the most desired jobs, and exquisite artisans are revered within their communities.

- **Trade by Town:** The Cunei Myrgha are proud of the specific trades or artisanry that their town produces, and they take great pains to retain fine artisans within their town. In the Myrgha way, this means there are displays of incredible wealth shown for the finest artisans of the land, and they are given a great deal of wealth from their communities to demonstrate their skill.

## Values

- **Hard Work:** The Cunei Myrgha value hard work, and working on something until it is done.

- **Artistry:** The Cunei Myrgha value beautiful arts and artistry - most especially fine carvings and statue work - and artisans are highly prized and revered within Cunei Myrgha communities.

- **Simplicity:** The Cunei value simplicity, which to them goes hand in hand with fine craftsmanship. The Cunei do not create complex gear contraptions -they create perfect works of simple art.

- **Letter of the Law:** In spite of the apparent gambling addiction of many Cunei Myrgha, the Cunei still hold true to the letter of the law, and are very proud of the strange, byzantine bureaucracy of the Cunei.

## War

- **Land Forces:** The Cunei Myrgha are not traditionally competent sailors like the Myrgha or other nearby cultures, and they have a more focused footsoldier military - descended in part from their history as front line fodder of the Myrgha.

- **War Machines:** The Cunei do not create complex contraptions, but are capable of fine crafts in war. They have created the largest ships, and the most exceptionally crafted siege engines, equipped now in the Myrgha armies Plot Hooks: Cunei Myrgha.

- **Tension:** The Myrgha-fueled tension between the Black and White Cuneises, and their corrupt pseudo-oligarchic figureheads, creates a tense landscape of political intrigue and murders.

- **Myrgha War:** The Myrgha constantly interfere in Cunei affairs to retain good deals, values, and control over Myrgha agricultural and marble works upon which they depend. The Ministry of Policy dedicates a broad swath of effort to wrangling Myrgha influence, and defeating it.

- **Gambling Ring:** An illegal gambling ring has sprouted up within the region. Players may take on either side - the Ministries investigating and trying to clamp down on the ring, or the sporting Cunei Myrgha seeking to claim the ultimate prize (a finely crafted, magical sword) before the Ministries close in on the operation.

## Subculture: White Cuneis

The descendants of the White Queen, and the more industrial of the two Cuneises, the White Cunei live in a drier, arid climate, and are more dedicated to mining and artisanry than their eastern cousins.

### Architecture

- **Large Size:** The legacy wealth of the White Cuneises is apparent in their cities, which are larger than the Black Cuneis cities. In general, White Cuneis cities are more urban, with narrower streets, and taller multi-stories buildings.

- **Carved Cities:** The greatest Carved City of the White Cuneis is Whitecliff, which is also the largest White Cuneis city, and the first carved city. Horenol and Harid, the other major White Cunei cities, are built instead with stacked white marble.

- **Iron Domes:** Because of the close proximity between Kish and the White Cuneis, the White Cunei often incorporate iron domes into palacial or resplendant buildings - especially the White Palace in Whitecliff.

### Economy and Trade

- **Master Artisans:** The White Cuneises are known for simple, but perfectionist, artisanship. They create a multitude of produced goods, with more famous examples being pottery, marble works, carvings, and ironworks.

- **Source of Marble:** The White Cuneises are a massive source for marble and chalk for the Myrgha, and the Marble Artisans and Miners who produce chalk and marble are firmly in the pocket of the Myrgha.

### Folklore and Superstitions

- **Folk Spirits:** There are many more Marblekin in the White Cuneis than in the black, leading to complex customs surrounding how to respond and respect Marblekin existing within the Cuneises.

- **The Spirit of Gongshuansen:** The Cunei share the Myrgha tradition of anointing an Oligarch as a “descendant of the White Queen” (the last Myrha Oligarch of the Cuneises). To combat the corruption, the Cunei anoint a descendant in her line, give them immense quantities of gold, and treat them as a divine figurehead.

### Food

- **Seafood:** The White Cuneis has a great tradition of seafoods and garums, and the White Cunei are greatly dependent upon fish harvested from the western borders of Cunei lands.

- **Salt and Spice:** The cuisine of the White Cuneis tends to be saltier and spicier than the Black Cuneises. A famous dish in the White Cuneis is Red Stew, a fish, hominy, and chile stew that is meant to be as spicy as possible.

### Government

- **One Oligarch:** There is only one Oligarch for the Cunei Myrgha - a descendant of the White Queen in Whitecliff. This figurehead emperor is forever annointed with gold, and serves a number of ceremonial roles within the White Cuneis for the Ministries there.

- **A Separated Ministry:** The White Cuneis is a distinct governmental entity from that of the Black Cuneis, and the Ministry of the White Cuneis is centered on the government buildings in Whitecliff that make up part of the White Palace complex.

- **Corruption and Ministries:** The Ministries of Whitecliff are most corrupt when associated with Marblework, Ironworks, and Mining. The Policy Ministry has broadly overtaken the others as a controlling force, dedicated to weeding out this corruption.

## Subculture: Black Cuneis

The historically poorer region of the Cuneises, post-Myrgha separation it has become by far the richer, due to Myrgha dependence on food supplies. Unfortunately for most Cuneises, the wealth of the region concentrates into the hands of a few.

### Architecture

- **Smaller Cities:** The Black Cuneises are historically smaller and more rural than their White Cunei counterparts. Though Heinol is the largest Black Cunei city, it is significantly smaller than Whitecliff.

- **Carved Cities:** The Black Cuneis cities of Heinol and Hindren are both carved cities, made of black marble. Most of the other Black Cuneis cities are made of clay and wood, as historically, the rarer black marble has all been shipped to Myrghos.

- **Terraced Homes:** Small towns often have large, ornate, black marble plantation houses, where the rich ministers of agriculture and farmcrops live. These homes are often terraced, surrounded by the terraced croplands they inhabit. Recent modern Black Cunei architecture has followed along the similar terracing trend, with multi-leveled homes.

### Economy and Trade

- **Raw Goods:** The Black Cuneises are most known for their agricultural center, and are the largest regional producers of raw goods such as foodcrops, dyes, and peat for the Myrgha and Cuneises, as well as fragrant soaps. The rich black soils of the Black Cuneis are highly fertile for crops.

- **Rare Marble:** The largest Black Marble mines of the Black Cuneises outside of the cities of Heinol and Hindren are smaller than the White Cunei mines, and have been entirely sold to an Oligarchic Myrgha family. As a result, the Black Cunei don’t have access to their own marble, except that rare marble which can be carved from their own cities.

- **Corruption and Food:** The Agricultural Ministers who are meant to support the Black Cuneis are the most corrupt, and wealthy, members of all the Cuneises, through backhanded deals with the Myrgha.

### Folklore and Superstitions

- **Goat Eaters:** The famed Cunei Goat Eaters are the spirits of the dead who come back, and survive on goat’s blood. They are common in the Black Cuneis, and families often lead offerings of Goat Blood for them in front of pastures, to ward them from harming living goats.

### Food

- **Terraced Paddies:** Terraced paddies have been set up all along the wide slopes of the Black Cuneis, changing the landscape and promoting agriculture in the region. These terraced paddies, introduced from eastern Myrghos, farm taro, rice, and agave.

- **Forever Stew:** The Black Cuneises have a tradition of maintaining a single pot of stew, and continuously adding new ingredients to the boiling stew forever. The oldest of these, in Hearth, is already over a hundred years old.

### Government

- **No Gods, Only Men:** The Black Cuneis Government is a distinct Ministry from the White Cuneises, and is characterized by its complete disconnect from the tradition of Myrgha Oligarchy. Instead, the Oligarchy appoints a “Minister of Gold” from the Ministry of Economy to lead crucial ceremonies and decisions. This minister does not “own” the gold, and thus is not at risk of attracting a spirit of Gongshuan.

- **Corruption and Ministries:** The Black Cuneises are a tangle of corruption. Their relative geographic closeness to the Myrgha island chain, their tangle with the Myrgha Oligarchy over their large Black Marble mines, and the rampant Myrgha corruption of the Ministry’s agricultural ministers has left the Black Cuneises a tangle of influences and corruption.
