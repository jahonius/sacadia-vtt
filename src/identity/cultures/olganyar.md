# The Olganyar


The pungent odors of cardamom, cranberry, ambergris, rotting fruits, chicken blood, and burning goat feces fill the air of Eastim Market, radiating from the pyre of a merchant’s altar. Each merchant gondola has its own signature smell in hopes that the God will recognize, and bless, their travels. The canals reek with unique scents, boats passing between buildings with seeming disregard to the stench.

## Pirate Guilds

The Olganyar are a cunning and crafty naval people, building their cities on canal waterways using the hulls of old ships. They are most known for:

- **The Guilds:** The Olganyar use a guild-based system of govenrment, convening a council of guilds known as the Runar to govern the affairs of their people.

- **Scented Ships:** Olganyar believe that the God of Travel must track caravans by scent, or they will lose his favor (and good fortune). As a result, merchant ships and travelers of the Olganyar reek of unique scents, to retain the favor of their travel God.

- **Fjord Canals:** The Olganyar build cities atop mountainous fjords, carving channeled waterways into the stony ground which freeze over completely in winter, but afford ship travel in summer.

- **Ancient Ruins:** The Olganyar inhabit Olgan’s Pass, a historically rich, cursed, and dangerous landscape dotted with the ruins of a now dead culture, the Olemaic.

## The Cursed Fjordlands

Nestled into the Byzantine Fjordlands of Olgan’s Pass, the Olganyar have settled into a still-cursed piece of history. Olgan’s Pass, for centuries the border between east and west in Cenaris, is dotted with the ancient ruins of the East Olgan Empire, an ancient culture of theocratic mysteries. These ruins hold many secrets and many dangers. The Olganyar have settled right in regardless. Set into a previously advantageous position, the Fjordlands no longer offer a beneficial trade location with the rise of Kish to the west, and the Olganyar now form the westernmost tip of a chain of roads stretching east to the lands of the Dianni. The Olganyar are a naval peoples, instead, forever looking south towards the Myrgha with an uncomfortable stare.

### A History of the Olganyar

Descended from a mix of cultures, the Olganyar origin story told to children would tell that the Olganyar are descended from Olemaic, who survived the Fontspill and lived on small islands to the west. After the Font’s growth, eventually these Olemaic sailed eastward, where their last king was killed by the Myrgha in an act of betrayal. The Myrgha then attempted to kill all Olganyar, who sailed northwards to try to escape. The Olganyar eventually escaped the Myrgha by sailing up-river at the northern edge of the Ardus Yauga, losing the Myrgha in a byzantine network of fjords and rivers into which the Myrgha would not sail, due to the many plauging curses and superstitions upon the land. Due to the relative protections afforded the Olganyar (curses being less directly malicious than the Myrgha) they settled right in to a cursed existence.

### Olgan’s Pass

Olgan’s Pass, the home of the Olganyar, is dotted with the ruins of old East Olem, and the growing, often cursed cities of the Olganyar.

### The City of Maugar

The city of Maugar, the capitol of the Olgnayar, is a thriving city built upon a fjord. Using the Olganyar method of architecture (capitalising upon retired ship hulls to form the exteriors of buildings), the Olganyar build their city right onto the fjord, forming a network of byzantine canals. Settled into a relatively safe region of Olgan’s Pass, Maugar is known for its canals, which freeze solid into streets in the winter, and for the thriving (but smelly) markets just outside the city.

### The Dread Pits

One of the ruins of the old East Olem has a pulsing, massive growth within it - a growing flesh titan of unknown machinations, crawling with haunted undead. Rather than preocupying themselves with this strange pit, the Olganyar built a prison over it, and throw those with life sentences into the Dread Pits. Any who escape receives a full commuting of their sentence. None ever do.

### The Spire Gates

The Gates at the Northern Edge of Olgan’s Pass, the Spire Gates are a city built into a wall. Though much smaller than the nearby Tianqi walls, the Spire Gates are known as a ranger outpost manned by a distinct group of Olganyar from the Adventurer’s Guild, who spend their days plumbing into the ruins beyond. These ruins, called Teko Aua, were once the capitol city of East Olem. Today, they are deeply cursed ruins, filled with traps (such as mercury pools and triggered rockfalls), as well as haunting undead.

# Cultural Tapestry

## Architecture

- **Buildings:** Many buildings are built around old ships which have been ‘retired’ (no longer seaworthy) as a core construction element. As a result, much of the architecture of the Olganyar is partly old ship hulls.

- **Decorations:** Many buildings have large, sail-like flags and banners to make buildings look more like ships, painted in vibrant colors.

- **Town Layout:** All government offices are required to be ‘in town’, while no trade or exchange of goods can happen within the city. Over time, this has meant that city interiors have become poorer and poorer, while bordering regions officially outside city borders have become more lively and rich.

## Arts and Music

- **Cultural Colors:** The primary colors of the Olganyar culture are Cranberry Red, Foggy Blue, and Bright Golden Yellow. Banners of these colors are common and wave throughout their cities.

- **Plays and Shows:** Bards describe detailed stories while silent actors in colorful clothing play them out behind the bards. Romantic tragedies are the most common form of show, punctuated at intermissions by slapstick-style silent comedies.

- **Music:** Drums are common, as are lead pipe instruments. The lead in the pipes slowly drives their musicians insane, and music of this kind is often frenetic and intense.

## Customs and Language

- **Language:** The language of the Olganyar is Bushiu, the trade language of the Ardus Yauga.

- **Holidays:** The most popular holiday among Olganyar is Lover’s Day, a large celebration in which engaged couples steal their dowries from the family of the prospective husband. Olganyar cities devolve into chaos as heists happen simultaneously across town.

## Economy and Trade

- **Uniqe Scents:** For successful trade, it is tradition to always make caravans smell strongly. The goal of this practice is to create a unique smell for your caravan or ship, so that the God of Travel can always find you. Wealthy traders make their caravans smell fragrant and lovely, while poor caravans must make do with making their caravans smell strongly badly.

- **City Restrictions:** Olganyar cities restrict all bargains, deals, or exchange of money within city limits. As a result, sprawling markets are often found just offshore, or just ouside city limits.

## Family Structure

- **Courtship:** Dates are an opportunity for a challenge. Both partners gather to identify how each participant performs under stress through a dangerous activity. Examples can be a hunt, a trip, or a fight they try to start together.

- **Engagement:** When a couple is engaged, they must steal a dowry from one of their families through an elaborate and previously planned heist. Typically, these dowries are easy to steal. In rare occasions where a family’s parents do not want the wedding to occur, they will make the dowry difficult or dangerous to obtain.

## Folklore and Superstitions

- **Folk Fae:** The most common of Folk Fae are Flonks of Honkers, which steal scents and are considered bad luck for trade.

- **Wooden Totems:** Large wooden totems are constructed to watch over the entrances of important buildings, or at entrances to towns or regions. These totems are meticulously carved to represent gods who watch over the area.

## Food

- **Unique Cuisine:** A unique food item is Aostel, a salted and fermented poisonous fish which is wrapped in seaweed and put under a rock on shore for a year until it is no longer poisonous.

- **Common Crops:** Taro, cranberry, yams, wormwood, and chestnut.

## Games and Leisure

- **Kettleskull:** The most popular sport among the Olganyar. Large poles are set into the water, with a human skull on top of the tallest pole on either side of the arena. Two sets of teams compete to grab their opponent’s skull and return it to their home base without getting knocked off into the water.

## Government

- **Structure:** The council of guild representatives is called the Runar, and the leader of the council is the Matarunar. The Matarunar is voted in an election by all guilds. No Runar member can vote for themselves.

- **Criminal Punishment:** Crimes cannot be punished by fines because money cannot be exchanged inside the city. Therefore, minor crimes are typically punished with forced labor, while major crimes are punished by execution via ‘pit’, where travelers are sent to an Olganyar execution ground to fight monsters.

- **Crimes:** Digging has become a crime in an attempt to stop the creation of canals to remove parts of the city. Digging now occurs surreptitiously and at night.

## Religion

- **Protectors:** . Each city’s “City God” protets that city and watches over it as a protectorate.

- **Death:** Bodies are cremated except for the skull, which is retained. Poems to the dead are written in cloth and sewn into the skulls, which are then placed on top of urns which contain the ashes of that person. Remains are typically interned into hollows carved into the side of cliff faces facing the sea.

- **Guides:** Each individual has a personal god, which is decided for them as a guiding aspect when they come of age. For the rest of their life, they work to live up to that God’s moral truths and expectations.

## Societal Structure

- **Guilds:** Guilds are the primary structure of governance and society throughout Olgan. Each guild has distinct hierarchies – your rank in the guild is a social stratification. The guild system is not egalitarian. ½ to ¾ of your wealth when you die goes to your guild, so guilds retain significant power over their constituents.

## Values

- **Personal Wealth:** Greater wealth is perceived as a key value among the Myrgha, producing social mobility and the ability to manifest your own destiny.

- **Communal Support:** Values are arranged around personal values, then family, then guild, then all Olganyar. Independence is key then to all Olganyar.

- **Myrgha:** All Olganyar are raised to hate the Myrgha, and the Olganyar have a cultural vendetta against Myrgha. They are the only group upon which vengeance is considered an acceptable calling.

## War

- **Cassus Belli:** War is not conducted for glory, but for material gain. Revenge is the poorest form of reasoning. A good ‘cassus belli’ is that ‘that culture has something we want’, not ‘that culture has wronged us’.

- **Organization:** Overarching tax from all guilds goes to paying soldiers. Guilds are taxed according to their means (and can pay with service, gold, or materials that can support society through complex tax laws)

- **Social Mobility:** Citizens can join the military for several years. By serving for years, a citizen is enabled to jump several rungs in the guild hierarchy upon leaving, providing incentive for young men and women to join.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Dread Pits:** Players are sentenced to die in the Dread Pits, Maugar’s most esteemed criminal pit, and must fight their way through an undead dungeon to live!

- **The Digs:** Smugglers often dig tunnels under the Eastin and Eastout Markets to evade import taxes. These tunnels have become byzantine and dangerous with flooding and sinkholes. If a sinkhole opens up, it may be difficult to find your way back out!

- **Lover’s Day:** One member of the party is engaged, but their lover’s family detests them! And so the dowry must be claimed through a difficult dungeon that the family has set up. The party must work together to claim the dowry, allowing them to marry the love of their life.
